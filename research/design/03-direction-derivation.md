# Visual direction: derivation

Process note: this follows the Impeccable `new-work` flow. The point of writing it down is that the *derivation* is auditable — the direction has to be defensible as a decision, not presented as taste.

## The mechanism, in one sentence

Hermes Agent writes down what it learns as reusable procedures, remembers across sessions, and hands work to copies of itself — so it accumulates competence instead of restarting from zero each time.

## The audience's real scene

A practitioner at a laptop, terminal open, deciding whether to trust a program that modifies its own instructions. Two of the three tracks read this as an engineering question; the third reads it as an accountability question. All three live in plain-text files, YAML, logs, and version numbers.

## The rut, named so it can be avoided

The category default is unmissable: **Hermes → Greek messenger god → dark surface, engraved serif display, gold or neon accent, caduceus mark, mono labels.** It is the literal reading of the product's name, which is exactly the reflex to distrust — and it collides with Impeccable's named AI-convergence cluster (near-black + one accent + glowing edges; or editorial hairlines + italic display serif + tracked mono labels).

Its predictable opposite is equally a rut: **the friendly light-mode developer-docs site** — white ground, blue accent, rounded cards, illustration of a robot.

Both are excluded from the candidate list except for the single allowance the rules give the brief's own picture: one candidate spent on the classical reading.

## Seven grounded visual systems

Drawn from the graphic and procedural traditions this audience actually reads, ordered by resonance — meaning *known by heart* **and** *able to carry the mechanism*. Material families span record-keeping, notation, technical illustration, signal infrastructure, and inscription.

**1. The aviation checklist and quick-reference handbook.** Laminated procedure cards, challenge-and-response format, "memory items" versus read-and-do, abnormal-procedure tabs. Resonance: highest. A skill file *is* a written procedure that exists so nobody re-derives it under pressure; the approval prompt *is* challenge-and-response; and for the governance reader, the checklist is the original audit artifact.

**2. The orchestral full score.** Many staves on one time axis, parts entering and resting, rehearsal marks, a conductor reading vertical simultaneity. Resonance: very high — the field already borrowed the word "orchestration," and no other notation shows parallel work against shared time as clearly. Carries delegation, subagent fan-out, and the Kanban board.

**3. The exploded-view assembly instruction.** Numbered steps, prior assembly ghosted, new parts bright, 1:1 call-outs, wordless arrows, a module grid ruling alignment. Resonance: high — universally legible, and it is a *teaching* grammar rather than a decorative one. Carries the curriculum's sequence better than anything else on this list.

**4. The bound laboratory notebook and its protocols.** Paginated, dated, signed and witnessed; protocols printed then corrected by hand in the margins across runs; results tipped in; nothing erased, only struck through and re-entered. Resonance: high. This is the only candidate that carries *all three* of the product's hard parts at once — a procedure that improves through use (the skill and its revisions), a record that accumulates (memory and session history), and an accountability trail that cannot be quietly rewritten (the governance reading).

**5. The telephone patch bay and switchboard.** Jack fields, cords, operator positions, routing made physical. Resonance: medium-high for the messaging gateway across twenty-odd platforms and for MCP/model routing — but it says nothing about learning, which is the product's actual centre.

**6. The ship's log and pilot book.** Dated entries in a bound log; sailing directions accumulated from voyages; corrections pasted in as notices arrive. Resonance: real but partly borrowed romance — this audience does not read these by heart, and it overlaps candidate 4's territory while carrying less of it.

**7. Greek epigraphy — the inscribed stele.** Lapidary capitals cut on a stoichedon grid; laws published in stone specifically so citizens could hold officials to them. Resonance: the one candidate spent on the brief's own picture, and its honest strength is the *stele as public accountability instrument* rather than the god as mascot. Ranked last because the audience does not live in it: it is an aesthetic import, and it is the rut.

## Assignment

`concept-seed --scope direction --mode read` → key `29e812a8`, **assigned index 4: the bound laboratory notebook and its protocols.**

The assignment's purpose is to refuse my own ranking reflex — left alone, every run of this exercise ships candidate 1. I have no factual grounds to re-roll: the notebook carries the product's truth and the reading task completely. Taste is explicitly not grounds.

**Dealt challengers worth fusing** (from the catalog, weighed on audience identification and product clarity):

- *Brick build-instruction booklet* — numbered exploded steps on a ruled ground, ghosted prior assembly, bright new part, wordless arrows. Effectively the catalog's version of my candidate 3, and a strong one: it is the clearest teaching grammar available. Survives.
- *Ghost-sign gable palimpsest* — decades of hand-painted advertisements fading over one another, older strata surfacing as newer paint fails; depth is time. Genuinely resonant with memory strata and with the way old skills persist under new ones. Survives as an alternate.
- *Teletext service* — three-digit page addresses keyed directly, a rigid character cell grid, block-mosaic graphics, REVEAL hiding answers. Resonant with terminal content and with progressive disclosure, but carries a real risk of reading as retro costume rather than as a system.
- Rejected on weighing: liquid-light ballroom (illegibility is the opposite of the reading task), dry-brush storm panels (beautiful, carries nothing of the mechanism), webring homepage lane (nostalgia would undercut the credibility this project exists to establish).

## The tension that must go to the user

The user pinned **mythic-tech** — candidate 7 — before any research existed, choosing from a menu I wrote. A pinned direction beats the roll, always; that is their call and not mine to overturn.

But two things have changed since the pick, and it would be dishonest to build on it without saying so. First, the rules I have since adopted identify that pick as the literal reading of the product name and adjacent to a named AI-convergence cluster — the exact reflex the process is designed to catch. Second, the research revealed what the product actually is, and the classical reading turns out to carry the *least* of it while the notebook carries the most.

So the decision goes back to the user with visual comps rather than adjectives, presenting the assigned direction fully committed alongside their pinned option and the surviving challengers. If the pin stands, it stands — and then it gets built from the full material range of inscription and public record, not from the softest rendition of it, which is where every model lands by default.
