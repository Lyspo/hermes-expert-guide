The official documentation is where most of what follows comes from. It is
accurate, it is thorough, and it is arranged for lookup, which is the right shape
for reference material and no help at all when the question is what to learn
second.

This lesson is the map: every subsystem named once, the module that teaches it,
and what is left out with the reason attached to each line.

## What it costs

Fifty-one lessons. 611 minutes of written material if you read every word on
every track, which nobody does — the tracks exist so nobody has to. The figure
that matters is the core path your track is asked to finish.

| Track | Core lessons | Core path |
|---|---:|---|
| Newcomer | 33 | 382 min · 6 h 22 m |
| Operator | 46 | 554 min · 9 h 14 m |
| Architect | 35 | 429 min · 7 h 09 m |

In ten-to-thirty-minute sittings, the Newcomer path is thirteen to thirty-eight of
them. That number belongs at the start rather than being discovered somewhere
around module 6.

<MarginNote>
The docs' learning path also offers six routes by use case, each a numbered
reading order. They are genuine sequences. What they do not carry is an argument
for the order, or a sentence telling you what to skip.
</MarginNote>

The docs set three tiers against those figures: Beginner in about an hour,
Intermediate in two to three, Advanced in four to six. The comparison flatters
neither side until you look at what each measures. That Advanced tier is four
pages, and two of them are about working on Hermes rather than using it. Longer
here means further, not slower.

## What is left out, and why

The test throughout: does this teach a mechanism you will reason with, or is it a
surface you can look up? Mechanisms earn lessons. Surfaces earn a cheatsheet row.

- **Every messaging platform but one.** One gateway process, one authorization
  chain, identical mechanics behind each adapter. Telegram is taught end to end;
  the rest is a capability matrix you consult once.
- **The skills catalogues.** Two reference pages naming hundreds of skills.
  Module 6 teaches how to search them and judge what comes back.
- **The domain integrations** — Home Assistant, Spotify, finance, blockchain,
  gaming. The pattern is one idea, a skill wrapping a command-line tool, taught
  once in module 6. Two hundred instances of one pattern are not a curriculum.
- **Batch trajectory generation and RL training.** Real, documented, and not part
  of the running agent: it produces training data, a different job from operating
  one. No source in this guide's research confirms it was used in any published
  training run.
- **`hermes lsp`, `hermes computer-use`, the Atropos RL environments.** Named
  here, taught nowhere — no bearing on the model you now hold.
- **Nix, Termux, the Windows feature gaps.** Tier 2 or best-effort by the docs'
  own classification, and for Nix the reason is stated plainly: it "breaks often
  due to node.js packaging woes." Module 2 prints the tiers so an affected reader
  knows where they stand, and says the docs never enumerate what Windows loses.
- **Nous Portal prices.** Two direct fetches of the portal returned HTTP 429, so
  every figure available here is a search snippet. The tiers are named; no dollar
  amount appears anywhere.
- **Contributing to the core**, the contributor shell helpers, and the cosmetics —
  skins, themes, one decorative subcommand family. Both core-contribution pages
  open by telling you not to take that route.

<Depth min="operator">

Three surfaces are included but demoted, because each re-exposes the same agent
through a different door rather than adding a mechanism: the ACP editor
integration, the OpenAI-compatible API server, and the dashboard with its desktop
counterpart. They share one survey lesson in module 9.

Their governance-relevant facts do not wait for it. The dashboard's auth gate
fails closed — with no provider registered it refuses to bind rather than binding
open — and its middleware skips `/api/plugins/` routes entirely, so a non-loopback
bind exposes every plugin route unauthenticated. The API server requires a bearer
token on every deployment, loopback included. Those land in module 10, where they
change a decision.

Mixture of Agents is relocated rather than demoted. It reads like task
orchestration and is a model-selection strategy, so it sits in module 9 rather
than the multi-agent module, and its lesson opens by saying what it is not.

</Depth>

## Two commands with no page at all

`hermes project` and `hermes insights` both ship, both work, and neither has a
documentation page — confirmed by probing the URLs such a page would live at and
getting 404s from every one. Module 8 teaches `hermes project` from a docstring in
the source and labels it that way, because you deserve to know which facts here
came from documentation and which came from reading code.

That is the same test applied to the map itself, and it is why the exclusion list
will grow: the surface grows every week or two, and a guide that adds a lesson per
new page stops being a path and becomes a second set of documentation.
