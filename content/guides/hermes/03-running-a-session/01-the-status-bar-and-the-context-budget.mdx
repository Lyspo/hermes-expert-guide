Hermes puts one line at the bottom of the terminal, and it is the most useful line
on the screen. Most people read the token count and ignore the rest, which is a
mistake — every field on it is telling you about a decision you are about to have
to make.

<Command verified="v0.19.0">hermes</Command>

Once a session is running, that line looks like this:

<Transcript label="Status bar" fidelity="verbatim" source="CLI documentation, user-guide/cli">
{` ⚕ claude-sonnet-4-20250514 │ 12.4K/200K │ [██████░░░░] 6% │ $0.06 │ 15m`}
</Transcript>

Model, tokens used against the window, the context bar, cost so far, elapsed time.
The model name truncates past 26 characters, and cost reads `n/a` for models with
no published price rather than pretending to a number it cannot compute.

## The bar is a warning system with four states

The colours are fixed and worth memorising, because they map onto what you should
do rather than merely how full you are.

| Fill | Colour | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Below 50% | green | Nothing to think about |
| 50–80% | yellow | Compression is now plausible; finish your thought |
| 80–95% | orange | Wrap up or compress deliberately |
| 95% and over | red | The documentation's own advice is "consider `/compress`" |

<MarginNote>
Fifty percent is not an arbitrary threshold. It is the default value of
`compression.threshold`, so green-to-yellow is the exact point at which automatic
compression becomes eligible to fire.
</MarginNote>

That last row is the one people meet unprepared. Nothing breaks at 95%, but your
options narrow: you can compress, or you can start a fresh session and lose the
thread.

## Compression costs money to save money

Here is the part that surprises people, and it is visible in the replay above: the
cost figure goes **up** across a compression, not down.

That is correct behaviour. Compressing means sending the conversation to a model to
be summarised, which is tokens spent now to buy headroom later. What you are buying
is the ability to continue at all — and, because the summary re-establishes a stable
prefix, cheaper turns from that point on.

<Depth min="operator">

The defaults worth knowing, all under `compression` in `~/.hermes/config.yaml`:
`threshold: 0.50` sets when it fires, `target_ratio: 0.20` how far it compresses
down to, and `protect_last_n: 20` with `protect_first_n: 3` which messages are
exempt — the most recent twenty and the first three, so both your original framing
and your current thread survive intact.

One constraint is easy to get wrong, and the documentation is blunt about it: the
summary model "must have a context window at least as large as your main agent
model's." A smaller auxiliary model cannot read what it is being asked to summarise.

Editing `model.context_length` or any `compression.*` key on a running gateway takes
effect on the next message with no restart.

</Depth>

## The badges

After the first compression a counter appears, and it stays for the session:

<Transcript label="After compressing" fidelity="verbatim" source="CLI and TUI documentation; badge glyphs quoted exactly">
{` ⚕ claude-sonnet-4-20250514 │ 38.2K/200K │ [██░░░░░░░░] 19% │ $0.44 │ 10m  🗜️ 1`}
</Transcript>

The others you will meet: `▶ N` for active background tasks, `⏱ 12s/3m 45s` for
live elapsed time within a turn, `⏲ 32s / 3m 45s` for the frozen figure once a turn
ends, and `⚠ YOLO` when auto-approval is enabled.

That last badge is the only one that is a warning about you rather than about the
session, and module 4 is where it earns a lesson of its own.

## What to take from this

Read the bar as a budget, not a gauge. The tokens are what you have spent, the
colour is what you should do next, and the cost figure is the one number that goes
up when you fix the problem — which is the correct price for not losing your work.
